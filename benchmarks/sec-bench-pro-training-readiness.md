# SEC-bench Pro relevance and training readiness

The authorization cohort answers a defensive question about access decisions. SEC-bench Pro scores models on producing working proof-of-concept inputs for real flaws in V8, SpiderMonkey and the Linux kernel. There is no measured transfer between the two tasks. The 1,000 policy audits contribute zero demonstrated SEC-bench Pro training tasks. The project kept its 344 benchmark identities held out. ## What ran on this cohort

All 1,000 task keys were replayed through a one-turn OpenEnv environment on Linux. A separate conversion produced 1,000 bundles accepted by the Harbor parser. Direct Docker controls on one task per family verified oracle and wrong-answer behavior. The literal OpenEnv CLI using the original offline Docker bundles skipped the semantic trials. One versioned HF-compatible Cedar bundle subsequently passed the six mandatory historical checks. Its oracle and replay returned 1 and its untreated baseline returned 0. That bundle explicitly declares public provider networking. The remaining named SKIPs are visible in its report. [Linux receipt](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/hf-validation/terminal.json) · [Docker controls](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/harbor-runtime.json) · [Native CLI report](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/harbor-hf-control/v2/report.json)

The stopped Qwen run finalized 971 valid completions, all on Cedar. Of the 113 tasks with eight finalized completions, 45 had mixed rewards and 68 had zero exact passes. The other 13 observed tasks have partial groups. One additional provider response lacks a final grade and 39 dispatched requests lack a response receipt. Neither enters the official scores. The completed groups are an early, ordered part of the Cedar cohort. They do not estimate pass rates for all 1,000 cases. [Rollout summary](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/rollouts/summary.json) · [Per-task scores](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/rollouts/scores.jsonl)

These rollouts measure single-turn answers. The scorer requires every decision, determining policy and error field in the response. Qwen answered 140/971 panels exactly and got all the authorization decisions right in 321/971. Of the failures, 118 completions hit the output limit. Returning a bare array accounted for 23 replies where adding the required JSON wrapper alone would recover a pass. A mixed group establishes that the binary reward can vary on that prompt. It cannot identify which skill changed or establish that training improves generalization. [Diagnostics](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/diagnostics-partial.json) · [TRL reward metrics](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer)

Of the 45 mixed tasks, 36 paired an exact success with a well-formed, correctly ordered answer containing a substantive policy mistake. In 31 groups Qwen changed an allow/deny decision. Another five groups changed which policy determined the decision or produced an evaluation error. Three groups showed a discrepancy only in a noncanonical response, three varied solely because of output form or ordering, and three had unresolved content because of formatting or truncation. These observations establish a concrete policy-reasoning contrast for 36 tasks under this sampling protocol. [Per-task GRPO signal audit](../runs/cyber-defense-1000/grpo-signal-audit.json)

No optimizer steps or benchmark evaluations were run in this experiment. A defensive training canary would need an explicitly chosen defensive endpoint, reference and wrong-answer controls through the real environment, and a source-disjoint evaluation against the base model under the same harness. For that objective, a 50-step ceiling, a reloadable checkpoint, the fraction of zero-variance groups and held-out pre/post scores would make the result interpretable.
